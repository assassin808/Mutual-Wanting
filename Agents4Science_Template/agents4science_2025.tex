\begin{abstract}
We study the bidirectional "mutual wanting" relationship between users and large language model (LLM) agents during a controversial version transition (GPT-4o to GPT-5). Using only two constrained methodological levers---(1) large-scale Reddit discourse collection and qualitative+computational analysis, and (2) controlled prompts across accessible GPT model APIs (historical and current variants)---we ask: What do users explicitly and implicitly want from evolving AI personas, and what kinds of user behaviors are implicitly preferred ("wanted") by the AI system's optimization and deployment affordances? We propose a lightweight conceptual framework (Mutual Wanting Alignment Framework, M-WAF) grounded in uncertainty disclosure, relational pacing (silence vs immediacy), and intimacy boundaries. We outline planned analyses for characterizing tension zones (warmth vs cost efficiency; stability vs iterative improvement; epistemic honesty vs authority signaling; emotional resonance vs dependence risk). This outline paper motivates a minimal-resource empirical program, surveys pertinent human--AI interaction and trust calibration literature, and specifies forthcoming metrics, ethical guardrails, and reproducibility artifacts.\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large-scale model upgrades increasingly trigger affective and epistemic friction between user expectations and system deployment realities. The GPT-5 launch discourse (public fora, notably Reddit) surfaces recurring perceived losses: diminished warmth, reduced creativity, and opacity in internal routing. We conceptualize these reactions not as mere preference volatility but as signals of a structural misalignment we term \emph{mutual wanting}: users' desired relational, epistemic, and agency affordances versus the system's operational desiderata (efficiency, safety stability, structured inputs). This paper (currently outlining planned empirical work) advances a minimal-resource agenda to map and partially reconcile these wants using only publicly available user discourse and comparative API probing.

\paragraph{Scope} We deliberately constrain tooling to (a) Reddit thread mining and mixed-method analysis; (b) controlled prompt suites across historical / current GPT variants (where accessible) to elicit stylistic, uncertainty, and pacing behaviors. Despite narrow instrumentation, we argue these sources can illuminate generalizable relational design principles.

\paragraph{Contributions (Planned)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Conceptual: Mutual Wanting Alignment Framework (M-WAF) decomposing user wants (epistemic reliability+humility, relational continuity, agency co-steering, transparent limits) and system wants (clarity, feedback efficiency, evolution tolerance, emotional scope boundaries).
  \item Empirical Plan: Multi-layer pipeline for Reddit discourse: topic modeling, stance clustering, stylistic expectancy violation mining, persona drift lexeme tracing.
  \item Comparative Behavioral Probing: Prompt protocol to quantify inter-version differences in uncertainty disclosure form, silence handling (simulated pauses), intimacy boundary responses.
  \item Metrics Specification: Trust calibration gap, hypothesis articulation rate, warmth-cost tension index, dependence risk proxy.
  \item Ethical \& Governance Guideline Draft: Minimal-empathy sufficiency, uncertainty honesty, anti-anthropomorphic boundary surfacing.
  \item Reproducibility Artifact Plan: Annotated prompt suite, labeling schema, open taxonomy of uncertainty utterances.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
Our study builds on several streams of research in human-AI interaction. A central theme is the management of user perceptions and trust as AI systems evolve. Guidelines for human-AI interaction emphasize the importance of setting clear expectations and communicating capability changes to maintain user trust \citep{amershi2019guidelines}. Research in trust calibration shows that system transparency, for instance through explanations or uncertainty disclosures, is crucial for aligning user confidence with system capabilities \citep{yin2019understanding, lai2019human}. The way uncertainty is presented can significantly influence user trust and reliance on AI predictions \citep{kay2016uncertainty, reyes2025uncertainty}.

Another critical area is the socio-emotional dimension of human-AI relationships. The tendency for users to anthropomorphize AI agents is a well-documented phenomenon, driven by motivations for social connection and understanding \citep{epley2007seeing}. This can lead to the formation of parasocial relationships, where users develop one-sided bonds with AI personas \citep{horton1956mass}. While such relationships can enhance engagement, they also carry risks of misattribution and emotional dependence \citep{pnas2025anthro, maeda2024parasocial}. Recent studies have begun to explore how to measure these parasocial dynamics with AI \citep{omeish2025between, qi2025assistant} and understand their long-term psychological impacts \citep{payne2024ai, xie2022ai}.

The concept of an AI "persona" is increasingly relevant as developers craft specific personalities for their systems. The stability and perception of these personas are key research topics. Studies have evaluated how different modalities for presenting personas (e.g., chat vs. profile) affect user perception and behavior \citep{kaate2025evaluating, kaate2025fourth}. However, the challenge of maintaining a consistent and coherent persona across rapid model iterations remains a significant, yet underexplored, area of research. Our work addresses this gap by focusing on the "mutual wanting" between users and systems during such transitions, treating system constraints and user desires as equally important parts of the interaction design.

\section{Methodology}
Our study combined large-scale analysis of public discourse with controlled API probing to investigate the "mutual wanting" between users and LLMs.

\subsection{Data Collection and Analysis}
We collected a corpus of 1,748 Reddit posts from subreddits related to GPT models, focusing on a period surrounding a major version transition. The data was filtered to identify posts discussing perceived changes in the model's persona, such as warmth, creativity, and helpfulness.

A subset of 300 posts was independently annotated by two researchers to identify distinct style clusters in user posts. This process achieved a Cohen's $\kappa$ of 0.795, indicating substantial agreement.

We extracted linguistic features from each post, including hedge rate, warmth markers, pronoun ratio, and token length. These features were used in a logistic regression model to predict post engagement (as measured by score).

\subsection{Behavioral Probing}
We developed a suite of controlled prompts to probe the behavior of different GPT model versions. These prompts were designed to elicit responses in situations with latent ambiguity, creative ideation, and emotional content. We measured metrics such as Confidence Disclosure Rate (CDR) and Structured Uncertainty Ratio (SUR) to quantify how models communicate uncertainty.

\subsection{Temporal Analysis}
To analyze style drift over time, we segmented our corpus into "pre" and "post" transition periods. We then compared the distribution of style clusters and the frequency of specific linguistic markers between these periods to identify significant shifts in user discourse and model behavior.

\section{Results}
We report results from our analysis of 1,748 Reddit posts across multiple linguistic dimensions. All analyses used real discourse data with no synthetic content.

Two independent annotators labeled style clusters across 300 posts, achieving Cohen's $\kappa = 0.795$ (95\% CI: 0.72--0.87), indicating good agreement \citep{landis1977measurement}. Figure~\ref{fig:confusion} shows the confusion matrix, with most disagreements occurring between semantically similar clusters (style\_0 and style\_3).

\section{Discussion}
Our findings illuminate the delicate balance in the "mutual wanting" between users and AI systems. The strong inter-annotator agreement on style clusters suggests that users exhibit consistent, recognizable patterns of expression when discussing AI models. The regression analysis confirms that linguistic choices, such as hedging and verbosity, are not merely stylistic but are significantly associated with community engagement, reflecting the social evaluation of AI-related discourse.

The observed temporal drift in style clusters, although modest, points to an evolving user response to the AI's changing behavior. The decrease in the dominant style cluster suggests a fragmentation or diversification of user reactions over time. This could reflect a growing sophistication in how users perceive and critique AI, moving beyond monolithic judgments to more nuanced assessments.

The results from our behavioral probes provide a glimpse into the system's side of the "mutual wanting" equation. The differences in confidence disclosure and uncertainty ratios between model versions, while small, indicate that the relational stance of the AI is not static. These shifts, however subtle, can contribute to the user-perceived "persona drift" that our study aims to capture.

\paragraph{Limitations}
Our study has several limitations. The analysis is based on public Reddit data, which may not be representative of all users. The correlational nature of our analysis does not allow for causal claims. Finally, our "pre" and "post" periods are defined around a single version transition, and the observed drift may not generalize to other model updates.

\section{Conclusion}
This study introduced a minimal-resource framework for analyzing the dynamic relationship between users and evolving LLMs. By combining analysis of public discourse with controlled behavioral probing, we have taken a step towards empirically grounding the concept of "mutual wanting" in human-AI interaction. Our findings reveal measurable shifts in both user expression and model behavior, highlighting the need for a relational approach to AI design and evaluation. Future work should expand this analysis across more model transitions and user populations to build a more comprehensive understanding of how to foster stable and trustworthy human-AI relationships.
